### 1.4.1 Why Ethics Matter in Software Engineering

Software increasingly controls critical aspects of modern life:
- Medical devices
- Autonomous vehicles
- Financial systems
- Social media platforms
- Government systems

> **🔑 Key Insight:** Software engineers are not just technical workers—they are **professionals with ethical responsibilities** to society, employers, and the profession.

#### Historical Examples of Ethical Failures

| Case | Issue | Impact |
|------|-------|--------|
| **Therac-25** (1980s) | Radiation therapy machine software bug | Patients received lethal radiation overdoses; 6 deaths |
| **Volkswagen "Dieselgate"** (2015) | Software designed to cheat emissions tests | $30B+ in penalties; environmental damage |
| **Facebook-Cambridge Analytica** (2018) | User data misused for political manipulation | Privacy violations; regulatory fines |
| **Boeing 737 Max** (2019) | MCAS software flaws contributed to crashes | 346 deaths; global fleet grounding |

### 1.4.2 ACM/IEEE Code of Ethics

The Association for Computing Machinery (ACM) and IEEE Computer Society jointly developed the **Software Engineering Code of Ethics and Professional Practice**. It is the standard framework for ethical decision-making in the field.

#### The Eight Principles

| # | Principle | Summary |
|---|-----------|---------|
| **1** | **PUBLIC** | Software engineers shall act consistently with the public interest |
| **2** | **CLIENT AND EMPLOYER** | Act in the best interests of client and employer, consistent with public interest |
| **3** | **PRODUCT** | Ensure products meet the highest professional standards |
| **4** | **JUDGMENT** | Maintain integrity and independence in professional judgment |
| **5** | **MANAGEMENT** | Promote ethical approaches to management of software development |
| **6** | **PROFESSION** | Advance the integrity and reputation of the profession |
| **7** | **COLLEAGUES** | Be fair to and supportive of colleagues |
| **8** | **SELF** | Participate in lifelong learning; maintain professional competence |

#### Detailed Breakdown

**Principle 1: PUBLIC**

> "Software engineers shall act consistently with the public interest."

Key obligations:
- Accept full responsibility for your work
- Consider health, safety, and welfare of the public
- Disclose any dangers or risks
- Report violations of ethics
- Do not participate in projects with unethical intent

**Principle 2: CLIENT AND EMPLOYER**

> "Act in the best interests of client and employer, consistent with public interest."

Key obligations:
- Provide honest estimates and assessments
- Do not use confidential information for personal gain
- Understand conflicts of interest
- Disclose issues that could affect the project

**Principle 3: PRODUCT**

> "Ensure products meet the highest professional standards."

Key obligations:
- Ensure specifications are realistic and achievable
- Ensure adequate testing and quality assurance
- Maintain data privacy and security
- Provide thorough documentation
- Strive for high quality, cost-effective solutions

**Principle 4: JUDGMENT**

> "Maintain integrity and independence in professional judgment."

Key obligations:
- Maintain objectivity
- Disclose conflicts of interest
- Do not accept bribes or improper influence
- Base decisions on evidence and expertise

**Principle 5: MANAGEMENT**

> "Promote ethical approaches to management of software development."

Key obligations:
- Ensure realistic estimates and schedules
- Provide appropriate resources and support
- Establish processes for quality and ethics
- Protect employees from coercion

**Principle 6: PROFESSION**

> "Advance the integrity and reputation of the profession."

Key obligations:
- Promote ethical practices
- Contribute to public knowledge
- Support professional development of others
- Report unethical behavior

**Principle 7: COLLEAGUES**

> "Be fair to and supportive of colleagues."

Key obligations:
- Encourage fair treatment of all
- Provide constructive feedback
- Credit others appropriately
- Help colleagues develop professionally

**Principle 8: SELF**

> "Participate in lifelong learning; maintain professional competence."

Key obligations:
- Maintain knowledge of current developments
- Improve ability to produce safe, reliable products
- Accept only work consistent with competence

### 1.4.3 Professional Responsibilities

#### Core Responsibilities

| Responsibility | Description |
|----------------|-------------|
| **Competence** | Only undertake work you are qualified to perform |
| **Quality** | Deliver products that meet specifications and are safe |
| **Honesty** | Provide truthful estimates, reports, and communication |
| **Confidentiality** | Protect employer and client proprietary information |
| **Conflict of Interest** | Avoid situations that compromise objectivity |
| **Whistleblowing** | Report serious ethical violations when necessary |

#### Whistleblowing Considerations

When is whistleblowing justified?

```
✓ The harm to the public is significant
✓ Internal channels have been exhausted
✓ The evidence is clear and documented
✓ The whistleblower has considered personal consequences
```

> **🔑 Key Insight:** Whistleblowing is a last resort, but sometimes it is the ethical obligation.

Software engineers must consider the broader impact of their work.

| Domain | Ethical Considerations |

| **Privacy** | Data collection; user consent; surveillance |
| **Security** | Vulnerabilities; encryption; responsible disclosure |
| **Accessibility** | Designing for users with disabilities |

| **Environmental Impact** | Energy consumption; e-waste; carbon footprint |
| **Employment** | Automation; gig economy; worker displacement |
| **Misinformation** | Content moderation; fake accounts; echo chambers |

Software engineers work within a legal framework:

### 💡 Study Activity: Deep Dive

#### Activity 1: Ethical Dilemma Scenarios

Apply the ACM/IEEE Code to these scenarios:

**Scenario A:** Your manager asks you to ship a software product that you know has a security vulnerability. The deadline is tomorrow. What do you do?

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>
<strong>Apply Principle 1 (PUBLIC) and Principle 3 (PRODUCT).</strong> You have an obligation to disclose the risk. Options: escalate to higher management, document the issue, propose a mitigation plan. If ignored, whistleblowing may be required if public harm is significant.
</details>

**Scenario B:** A client offers you a "bonus" if you deliver ahead of schedule, but you would have to cut testing to meet it.

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>
<strong>Apply Principle 4 (JUDGMENT) and Principle 2 (CLIENT).</strong> Accepting would compromise professional judgment. You have a duty to inform the client of quality risks. The short-term gain would create long-term risk for the client.
</details>

**Scenario C:** Your company uses open source code in its product but doesn't comply with the license terms.

<details>
<summary>Click for answer</summary>
<strong>Apply Principle 6 (PROFESSION) and Principle 2 (EMPLOYER).</strong> Violating licenses is both unethical and illegal. You have a duty to inform management of the compliance issue and work toward resolution.
</details>
